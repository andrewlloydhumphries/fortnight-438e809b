Assumptions & rates▸

Read off the payslips

  • Base rate $55.04, and every shift pays 8.00 hours. Ordinary hours of 24.00 and 32.00 are clean multiples, and the 1 June shift splits 1.25 + 6.75 = 8.00.
  • Night loading: +25% on hours worked 20:00–06:00, weekdays only. That is what the "NIGHT RATE" lines are. An afternoon shift touches 20:00–23:00 — 3 hours × 25% × $55.04 = $41.28, the exact figure on all five of those lines. A night shift touches 7.25 loaded hours, so $99.76.
  • Weekend: time and a half. The "W/E SHIFT" line is the extra half: 1.25 hours × 50% × $55.04 = $34.40. Night loading does not stack on top of it.
  • Public holiday: double time plus a day in lieu, from the 1 June line.
  • Student loan 12% above $928.00 a fortnight; KiwiSaver 3.5%. Both match all three payslips.
  • The short afternoon (14:40–19:00) finishes before 20:00, so it earns no night loading at all — it pays flat base rate on a weekday, the same hourly as a morning. Its paid hours are assumed to be the full 4.33 clock hours with no unpaid break, which no payslip confirms yet.

Tax settings, 2026–27

  • Brackets 10.5 / 17.5 / 30 / 33 / 39% at $15,600 / $53,500 / $78,100 / $180,000. ACC earners' levy 1.75% capped at $156,641. IETC $520 on the ME code, abating above $66,000.
  • Against all three payslips this reproduces PAYE within two cents, so it is calibrated rather than estimated.

Where it is softer

  • None of the three payslips contains a plain morning shift or an ordinary Saturday, so the weekend rule rests on a single $34.40 line. Worth checking against a payslip with a real Saturday on it.
  • The 20:00–06:00 window fits every line, but 20:00–08:00 would too if morning shifts are exempt. If a morning ever shows a $13.76 night line, it is the wider window.
  • The roster is a plain Week 1 / Week 2 with no calendar dates, so public holidays are not detected. Tap a day label to mark it as a stat day; tap again to clear it.
  • Ignores annual, sick and study leave, overtime past 80 hours a fortnight, on-call and PDRP allowances.
